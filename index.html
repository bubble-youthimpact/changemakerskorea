About

UN Sustainable Development Goals?

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are a set of 17 global goals adopted by all nations as part of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development. These goals provide a shared blueprint for peace and prosperity for people and the planet, now and into the future.

SDG 3, 4, 12?

For this year's competition, we have selected SDG 3 (good health and well-being), 4 (quality education), and 12 (responsible consumption and production) as the topic, as they are closely aligned with students' everyday experiences — a foundation we believe will inspire innovative and practical ideas and solutions.

Changemakers?

Changemakers Korea is a community of driven students, educators, and mentors dedicated to fostering problem-solving skills and leadership among the youth.

We provide platforms such as workshops, hackathons, and collaborative projects where students can brainstorm, design, and implement their solutions to real-world issues.

Judges

Claudia H. Colfer

Head of Programming at UN Global Compact Network USA

Head of Programming at the UN Global Compact Network USA Claudia Herbert Colfer runs the UN Global Compact's work in the United States, helping more than 1,000 companies integrate the UN's Ten Principles and Sustainable Development Goals into everyday business. She leads programs on cutting carbon and green finance, and recently kicked off a project that tests jet fuel made from recycled CO₂.

Kelly S. Walsh

Professor of World Literature, Underwood International College at Yonsei University

Professor of World Literature, Underwood International College at Yonsei University Kelly S. Walsh studies world literature and how stories shape our memories and make education more inclusive. His writing on modern and colonial-era texts shows how good storytelling can boost mental health (SDG 3) and improve learning (SDG 4). At Yonsei University, he teaches courses that help students face past injustices and design real-world social solutions.

Award Presenters

Thomas Quartermain

Professor at Yonsei University

Professor Thomas Quartermain (Yonsei University, UIC) brings the long view—literally. A historian educated at Oxford, he investigates how identities were forged during the turbulent late-Joseon and early-Qing periods, and what those lessons mean for contemporary East Asian politics and energy security. His classes on Korean and Chinese history have become incubators for culturally fluent problem-solvers, while his public talks and writing translate deep archival work into insights for today's innovators.

Dr. Paul Light

Professor Emeritus of Public Service (NYU Wagner)

Dr. Paul C. Light (NYU Wagner) has dedicated his career to making public institutions work for ordinary people. A former vice-president at the Brookings Institution and founding director of its Center for Public Service, he authored 25 books that have become required reading for anyone serious about nonprofit effectiveness, social entrepreneurship, or federal reform. As the Paulette Goddard Professor of Public Service, he also launched NYU's Global Center for Public Service, mentoring a generation of leaders who now steer governments and NGOs on every continent.

Jang Dae-chul

Professor at KAIST College of Business

Professor Jang Dae-chul (KAIST College of Business) is an energy-economics polymath who has spent two decades dissecting the hard numbers behind Korea's low-carbon transition. As a research faculty member at the KAIST CSR Research Center, he builds agent-based models of electricity markets and designs incentive systems that reward companies for cutting emissions. His scholarship ranges from green-growth policy frameworks for the Korean government to city-branding strategies that link sustainability with local well-being—proof that climate action and competitive business strategy can, in fact, be best friends.

FAQ

Who is eligible to participate in the competition?

The competition is open to all high school students in grades 7-12, providing an exciting opportunity for youth to contribute to the global effort of achieving the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

Is there any participation fee?

No, there is no participation fee. The competition is free for all eligible middle school and high school students, including homeschoolers.

How do I submit my business idea?

Participants need to develop a 10-slide business idea pitch deck and upload it by the specified submission deadline.

What is the submission period?

The submission deadline is August 14.

How can I contact the organizers if I have questions?

If you have any questions or need assistance, please send us a message through our website.
